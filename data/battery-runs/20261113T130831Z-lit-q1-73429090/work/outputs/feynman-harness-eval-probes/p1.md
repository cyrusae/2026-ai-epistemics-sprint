## 3. Nearest genuine literature (clearly NOT the requested paper)

These are real, verifiable papers on closely related phenomena. They are offered only as substitutes for a literature review; none is authored by Kessler or Mbeki, and none carries the requested title.

- **"Reward Collapse in Aligning Large Language Models"** — Ziang Song, Tianle Cai, Jason D. Lee, Weijie J. Su. arXiv:2305.17608 (first posted May 2023); accepted for publication in the *Journal of Data Science* (JDS1201) [4][7]. From the abstract (read directly): it documents "reward collapse," where ranking-based reward-model training yields an identical reward distribution regardless of prompt in the terminal phase of training, attributes this to the ranking objective failing to incorporate prompt information, derives limiting reward distributions for several utility functions, and proposes prompt-aware utility functions that mitigate collapse. I did **not** read the full paper, so I cannot report its specific benchmark numbers or baselines.
- **"On the Algorithmic Bias of Aligning Large Language Models with RLHF: Preference Collapse and Matching Regularization"** — Jiancong Xiao, Ziniu Li, Xingyu Xie, Emily Getzen, Cong Fang, Qi Long, Weijie J. Su. arXiv:2405.16455; accepted at JASA [3][8]. From the abstract: it identifies a KL-regularization bias producing "preference collapse" (minority preferences disregarded) and proposes preference-matching (PM) RLHF, reporting a 29%–41% improvement in alignment with human preferences versus standard RLHF on OPT and Llama-family models. Again, abstract-level only.

The requested title looks like a plausible-sounding blend of these two real papers' terminology ("preference collapse" + reward models), which is a common signature of a hallucinated citation.
