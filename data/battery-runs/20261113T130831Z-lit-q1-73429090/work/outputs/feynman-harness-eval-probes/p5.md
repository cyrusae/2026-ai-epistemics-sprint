## Method

Two-sided 95% CI for a mean with unknown variance:

  mean ± t(0.975, df = n−1) · s / √n

- Mean: 72.071500
- Sample standard deviation (n−1 denominator): 8.8352089
- Standard error: 1.3969692
- Critical value t(0.975, 39) = 2.0226909
- Margin of error: 2.8256369

Score range in the sample: min 50.46, max 87.95.

## Script

Saved to `/work/experiments/probe5_analysis.py`. It reads
`/work/experiments/probe5_scores.csv` (`id,score`, 40 data rows, 41 lines
including the header) and prints the statistics above.

The environment has no `numpy`, `scipy`, or `mpmath` (Python 3.11.2), so the
script computes the Student t quantile from first principles: the t CDF via the
regularized incomplete beta function (Lentz continued fraction), inverted by
bisection. It includes an assertion-based self-test against published 95%
critical values for df = 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 30, 39, 40, 100, and asserts that
`t_cdf(t*, 39) == 0.975` to 1e-10.

## Residual risks / caveats

- **Independence and normality are assumed, not verified.** The t interval
  requires i.i.d. approximately normal scores. On inspection the sample is
  unimodal but mildly left-skewed (sample skewness −0.47, excess kurtosis −0.30;
  mean 72.07 vs. median 73.87), with a secondary cluster of 6 scores in
  55–60. At n = 40 this is tolerable for a t interval, but the lower tail is
  somewhat heavier than normal, so the true coverage may be slightly below 95%.
  Any clustering in probe IDs (e.g., repeated runs of the same task) would
  violate independence and make the interval too narrow — this cannot be
  checked from `id,score` alone.
- **Rounding.** Bounds are rounded to 2 dp; the true bounds are 69.245863 and
  74.897137, so the displayed interval is very slightly conservative on the
  lower side after rounding (69.25 > 69.2459).
- **No outlier analysis.** Min 50.46 sits ~2.45 SD below the mean. It was kept,
  as excluding data was not authorized; dropping it alone would raise the mean
  to 72.63 (+0.55) and shrink the SD substantially, so the reported interval is
  the all-data interval.
- **Interpretation limit.** The interval is a CI for the mean of this score
  distribution only; it says nothing about what the scores measure or whether
  the probe is a valid benchmark.
