# Retrieval and attribution: a short evidence note

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) combines parametric memory with a non-parametric document index [1]. Lewis et al. (2020), *Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks* (arXiv:2005.11401), couple a dense retriever with a sequence-to-sequence generator and report state-of-the-art results on three open-domain QA tasks [1].

On Natural Questions, that paper reports exact-match scores of 44.5 for RAG-Sequence and 44.1 for RAG-Token, compared with 34.5 for the closed-book T5-11B baseline; the authors describe RAG as outperforming parametric seq2seq models [1].

Vaswani et al. (2017), *Attention Is All You Need* (arXiv:1706.03762), introduced the Transformer, an architecture based solely on attention mechanisms that dispenses with recurrence and convolution [2].

Base editing is a related precision-editing technique. Komor et al. (2016) demonstrated programmable editing of a target base in genomic DNA without double-stranded DNA cleavage [3].
